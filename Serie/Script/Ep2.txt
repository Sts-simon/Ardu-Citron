The bomb can see the target.
Put a TV camera in the nose, and the bomb is no longer blind. (pause 0,5 s)
The camera sends a picture. A tracker finds the target in it, and measures how far it is from the center.
That distance is the error. Guidance turns it into commands, the fins move, and the target comes back to the center. (pause 0,8 s)
Keep the error at zero, and the bomb flies straight at what it sees.
Weapons like the Walleye lock onto the contrast of the target before launch. After release, no human is needed: fire and forget. (pause 0,8 s)
Weapons like the GBU-15 send the picture back by data link. An operator watches and steers: a man in the loop. (pause 0,8 s)
But a camera needs light and contrast. Night, smoke, fog: the picture is lost, and so is the target. (pause 0,8 s)
The guidance is a function of the image. (pause 0,6 s)
