The bomb can see heat.
Put an infrared sensor in the nose, and the bomb no longer needs daylight. <break time="0.8s"/>
It doesn't look for visible light, it looks for thermal contrast.
An engine, a power plant, a vehicle in the dark: all emit heat.
The tracker locks onto the thermal signature, guidance moves the fins, and the bomb follows the target. <break time="0.8s"/>
Night no longer offers protection.
Weapons like the AGM-65 Maverick IR can strike in total darkness. <break time="0.8s"/>
Fire and forget, even at night. <break time="0.8s"/>
But thermal imagery still has limits. Heavy rain, dense clouds, or artificial smoke can block the heat signature. <break time="0.8s"/>
The guidance is a function of the contrast. <break time="0.8s"/>
